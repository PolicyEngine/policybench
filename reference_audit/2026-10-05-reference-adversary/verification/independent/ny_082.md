Verifier: independent Opus 5.5 agent, 2026-10-06. No engine was imported and no simulation was run, because the machine was overloaded. Engine values come from the frozen probe at `verification/probes/ny_child_care_credit_scenario_082.json`. Law comes from the enacted bill text and the codified statute.

## Verdict

**CONFIRMED. The reference is wrong.**

- Reference: $667 (ESCC $307 + NY CDCC $360).
- Corrected value: **$1,187.61**, or $1,188 in whole dollars. That is ESCC $307 plus the new Tax Law § 606(c-2) credit of $880.61.
- Adversary value: $1,187.55 / $1,187.60. This agrees with mine to within $0.06. The gap comes only from its slightly different NY AGI ($117,594 against the engine's $117,585.15).

L.2026 ch.59 (S.9009-C / A.10009-C), Part A, was signed on 2026-05-28, before the 2026-07-03 freeze. It made three changes:

- It confined the old § 606(c)(1) credit (a percentage of the federal § 21 credit) to "taxable years beginning before January first, two thousand twenty-six".
- It added § 606(c-2), a refundable New York State child and dependent care credit for "taxable years beginning on or after January first, two thousand twenty-six". The credit is computed directly on capped qualifying expenses at 55% minus 0.00025 percentage points per dollar of NY AGI above $15,000, with a 4% floor.
- It took effect immediately (§ 5 of Part A).

policyengine-us 2.15.17 still applies the pre-2026 § 606(c) formula in 2026: 20% × 3.0 multiplier × federal credit, giving $360. Upstream main (4c900b6d, 2.29.11) has the same defect.

Every substantive claim in the adversary's verdict checks out against the bill text: the statute, the dates, the expense cap, the percentage formula and units, the floor and refundability. Its ESCC figure of $307 also matches the engine.

## 1. The law

### 1a. Enacted bill, signing and effective date

**Source.** NY Assembly bill page for S09009 (2025-26 session), Summary/Actions/Text. Fetched 2026-10-06 from https://nyassembly.gov/leg/?default_fld=&leg_video=&bn=S09009&term=2025&Summary=Y&Actions=Y&Text=Y. nysenate.gov sat behind a Cloudflare challenge for curl, so I parsed the raw bill text from the Assembly site and cross-checked it against nysenate.gov through WebFetch.

**Summary line:** "BILL NO S09009C SAME AS SAME AS UNI. A10009-C … sets forth a child and dependent care credit for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2026 (Part A) … establishes a protecting our wallets energy rebate (POWER) credit (Part FF)".

**Actions:** "05/26/2026 PRINT NUMBER 9009C … 05/27/2026 PASSED SENATE … 05/27/2026 passed assembly … 05/28/2026 DELIVERED TO GOVERNOR 05/28/2026 SIGNED CHAP.59". Publication date 2026-05-28, which is **before the freeze**.

**Bill § 1:** "Each component is wholly contained within a Part identified as Parts A through JJ. The effective date for each particular provision contained within such Part is set forth in the last section of such Part."

**Part A § 1** amends Tax Law § 606(c)(1). New text is underlined in the bill, and the struck "[A]" is replaced:

> "(1) [A] For taxable years beginning before January first, two thousand twenty-six, a taxpayer shall be allowed a credit as provided herein equal to the applicable percentage of the credit allowable under section twenty-one of the internal revenue code for the same taxable year …"

**Part A § 2** adds § 606(c-2):

> "(c-2) New York state child and dependent care credit. (1) For taxable years beginning on or after January first, two thousand twenty-six, an eligible taxpayer shall be allowed a credit as provided herein to enable the eligible taxpayer to be gainfully employed or a full-time student at an educational institution for any period of the taxable year. If the amount of the credit allowed under this subsection for any taxable year shall exceed the eligible taxpayer's tax for such year, the excess shall be treated as an overpayment of tax to be credited or refunded in accordance with the provisions of six hundred eighty-six of this article, provided, however, that no interest shall be paid thereon."

- **(2)(A) Eligible taxpayer:** "a resident individual as defined in paragraph one of subsection (b) of section six hundred five of this article who, during the taxable year: (i) is not a dependent of another taxpayer pursuant to section one hundred fifty-two of the internal revenue code; and (ii) is not a resident married individual filing a separate return unless such individual meets the conditions in paragraph four of subdivision (e) of section twenty-one of the internal revenue code."
- **(2)(B) Qualifying individual:** "an individual who: (i) is under the age of thirteen at the close of the taxable year or is physically or mentally incapable of caring for themselves during the taxable year; (ii) resides with the eligible taxpayer for more than one-half of the taxable year; and (iii) is claimed as a dependent pursuant to section one hundred fifty-two of the internal revenue code, or could otherwise be claimed as a dependent."
- **(2)(C) Earned income:** "the wages, salaries, tips and other employee compensation, and those items of gross income which are includible in the computation of net earnings from self-employment."
- **(2)(D)(i) Qualifying expenses:** "the sum of the amount incurred and paid in the taxable year directly by an eligible taxpayer for: a. services provided in and about the eligible taxpayer's residence to provide care for any qualifying individual … and b. non-overnight services provided outside of the eligible taxpayer's residence to provide care for any qualifying individual; provided, however, that amounts incurred or paid for which the primary purpose is educational shall not be included."
- **(2)(D)(ii)** excludes "a. any amounts paid whereby the taxpayer receives reimbursement or are paid from funds provided by a government entity, dependent care account, or other third party". It also excludes certain payments to dependents and to the taxpayer's own children under 19.
- **(2)(D)(iii) Expense cap:** "a. three thousand dollars, in the case of an eligible taxpayer with one qualifying individual; b. six thousand dollars … two …; c. seven thousand five hundred dollars … three …; d. eight thousand five hundred dollars … four …; and e. nine thousand dollars … five or more qualifying individuals. Provided, further, that an eligible taxpayer's qualifying expenses shall not exceed such eligible taxpayer's earned income … or in the case of a married eligible taxpayer filing a joint return, the lesser of the earned income of each spouse determined separately."
- **(2)(E) Applicable percentage:** "(i) fifty-five percent in the case of an eligible taxpayer with a New York adjusted gross income determined pursuant to section six hundred twelve of this article of fifteen thousand dollars or less; or (ii) fifty-five percent reduced by twenty-five hundred thousandths of a percentage point for each dollar of an eligible taxpayer's New York adjusted gross income determined pursuant to section six hundred twelve of this article in excess of fifteen thousand dollars. Provided, however, that the applicable percentage for an eligible taxpayer shall not be reduced below four percent."
- **(3) Amount:** "the product of the eligible taxpayer's qualifying expenses … and the applicable percentage … Provided, however, the credit allowed under this subsection shall be reduced by twenty dollars for each one thousand dollars by which the eligible taxpayer's New York adjusted gross income … exceeds seven hundred fifty thousand dollars."
- **(5):** "Any references to the internal revenue code in this subsection shall be to the internal revenue code as it existed prior to January first, two thousand twenty-five."

**Interaction with the federal CDCC.** There is none. (c-2) does not reference § 21's credit amount, rate or expense limit. It borrows only § 152 (dependency) and § 21(e)(4) (married filing separately), as those stood before 2025 under (5). The federal OBBBA rate of 20% at this AGI is therefore irrelevant for 2026.

**Part A § 4** amends NYC Admin. Code § 11-1706(e)(1) so the NYC child care credit is a percentage of "the credit allowed under subsections (c) and (c-2) of section six hundred six".

**Part A § 5:** "This act shall take effect immediately." Applicability comes from (c-2)(1)'s own "taxable years beginning on or after January first, two thousand twenty-six" and from (c)(1)'s "before January first, two thousand twenty-six".

### 1b. Codified statute (nysenate.gov, current revision dated 2026-09-04)

https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TAX/606, read through WebFetch on 2026-10-06:

- **§ 606(c)(1)** opens "For taxable years beginning before January first, two thousand twenty-six, …". The codification matches the bill.
- **§ 606(c)(1-a)** reads: "For taxable years beginning after two thousand seventeen, for a taxpayer with New York adjusted gross income of at least fifty thousand dollars but less than one hundred fifty thousand dollars, the applicable percentage shall be the applicable percentage otherwise computed under paragraph one of this subsection multiplied by a factor …" This only scales the paragraph (1) percentage. Since paragraph (1) grants no credit for 2026, (1-a) has nothing to scale. This is the 3.0 multiplier the engine applies.
- **§ 606(c)(1-b)** reads: "Notwithstanding anything in this subsection to the contrary, a taxpayer shall be allowed a credit … that would have been allowed absent the application of section 21(c) of such code for taxpayers with more than two qualifying individuals …" It applies only to more than two qualifying individuals, so it is irrelevant to this one-child household. This "notwithstanding" clause was not amended. Whether it keeps any pre-2026 credit alive for 3+ dependents is a drafting question, and it does not touch this cell.
- **§ 606(c-2)(1), (2)(E), (3), (5)** text matches the bill word for word.
- **§ 606(c-1)(1-a)(B)** reads: "For taxable years beginning on and after January first, two thousand twenty-six, and before January first, two thousand twenty-eight, a resident taxpayer shall be allowed a credit … equal to the sum of: (i) one thousand dollars times the number of qualifying children of the taxpayer aged three or younger, and (ii) five hundred dollars …"
- **§ 606(c-1)(1-a)(C)** reads: "… reduced (but not below zero) by sixteen dollars and fifty cents for each one thousand dollars by which the taxpayer's federal adjusted gross income exceeds the threshold amount." The threshold for single, head of household or surviving spouse is "seventy-five thousand dollars". This text comes from L.2025 ch.59 Part C. S.9009-C does not amend (c-1): I grepped the bill text, and its § 606 amendments are (c)(1), (c-2), (n), (n-2), (eee) and (uuu).

## 2. Recomputation for scenario_082 (tax year 2026)

**Household facts** (from `stage1_prompt.md` and `scenarios.csv`):

- Head of household, age 23, wages $100,195.
- Child age 1. Has dependent status; the prompt places both in one household and tax unit.

**NY child and dependent care credit, § 606(c-2):**

1. **Eligible taxpayer.** The head is a NY resident, is not anyone's dependent and does not file separately. She is eligible.
2. **Qualifying individuals.** The child is under 13, resides with the head and is a dependent, so there is 1 qualifying individual.
3. **Qualifying expenses.** Start from $9,600 of child care, which is non-educational care for a 1-year-old with no third-party payment. The cap for one individual is $3,000. Earned income is $100,195, above the cap. Qualifying expenses are **$3,000**.
4. **Applicable percentage.** The rate is 55 − 0.00025 × (117,585.15 − 15,000) = 55 − 25.6463 = **29.3537%**. This is above the 4% floor.
5. **Product.** $3,000 × 0.293537 = **$880.61**. Counting whole dollars of NY AGI ($102,585) gives $880.6125, the same to the cent.
6. **High-income reduction.** NY AGI is below $750,000, so there is none.
7. **Refundability.** Fully refundable under (c-2)(1). It is therefore not part of `state_income_tax_before_refundable_credits`, and that reference ($5,598.55) is unaffected.

**Empire State child credit, § 606(c-1)(1-a)(B),(C):**

- The base is $1,000 for a child aged three or younger.
- Federal AGI exceeds the $75,000 threshold by 42 whole $1,000 steps. Both the engine's floor and the IT-213 line-6 rule ("Round down … to the nearest $1,000") give 42.
- The reduction is 42 × $16.50 = $693, so the credit is **$307**. It is refundable. The engine's `ny_ctc` is $307, which is correct.

**Other NY refundable credits for 2026:**

- **NY EITC and supplemental EITC: $0.** The federal EITC is $0, because AGI and the roughly $20.6k of investment income are far above the limits.
- **Real property tax credit (IT-214): $0.** Household gross income is above $18,000.
- **College tuition credit: $0.** No expenses are listed.
- **Additional or supplemental ESCC: not law for 2026.** It is absent from the engine's 2026 list, and S.9009-C enacts none.
- **STAR credit (§ 606(eee)).** It requires a registered owner-occupied primary residence. Neither is listed, and unlisted booleans are false, so it is $0. The adversary reached the same conclusion.
- **POWER credit (§ 606(uuu), Part FF).** This is a new refundable $100 credit (head of household) "for tax year two thousand twenty-six". Eligibility turns entirely on **2024** facts: full-year NY residence in 2024, a timely 2024 return, 2024 NY AGI of $150,000 or less, and not being a dependent in 2024. The prompt states facts only for 2026 and says to treat unlisted facts as false. The engine also books its predecessor rebate in the eligibility year: `ny_inflation_refund_credit.py:16-17`, "The tax effect belongs to the eligibility year", policyengine-us PR #7995. Under both conventions POWER is **$0 in this 2026 cell**. The adversary did not raise it. I flag it only as a scope note (section 5); it does not change the verdict, since neither reading gives $667.

**Total state_refundable_credits = $307 + $880.61 = $1,187.61**, or $1,188 in whole dollars.

**Residual uncertainty, which does not affect the verdict:**

### Variables

1. **New `ny_cdcc_state_credit_expenses`** (TaxUnit, YEAR, defined for NY).
   - Start from `tax_unit_childcare_expenses + add(tax_unit, period, ["care_expenses"])`.
   - Subtract employer or dependent-care-account reimbursements if modelled (for example `dependent_care_assistance_exclusion`), per (c-2)(2)(D)(ii)(a).
   - Apply `min_` against `p.expense_cap.calc(count_cdcc_eligible)` and against `min_head_spouse_earned`.
   - Return 0 when `count_cdcc_eligible` is 0.
   - Do **not** start from `cdcc_relevant_expenses`, which already carries the federal § 21(c) cap of $6,000 for 2+ individuals and would undercut NY's $7,500 / $8,500 / $9,000 tiers.
2. **New `ny_cdcc_state_credit_rate`:** `max_(p.rate.min, p.rate.max - p.rate.phase_down_rate * max_(0, ny_agi - p.rate.phase_down_start))`.
3. **`ny_cdcc`.**
   - Keep the current body as the pre-2026 branch.
   - When `p.state_credit.in_effect`, return `eligible * max_(0, expenses * rate - p.reduction.amount * floor(max_(0, ny_agi - p.reduction.threshold) / p.reduction.increment))`.

   - Do **not** multiply by the federal `cdcc_rate`.
   - The reduction uses whole $1,000 steps because the statute has no "or fraction thereof". Revisit when the 2026 IT-216 instructions are published.
4. **Labels.** Mark `ny_cdcc_rate` and `ny_cdcc_max` (and the (1-a) multiplier and (1-b) caps) as pre-2026 § 606(c) only.
