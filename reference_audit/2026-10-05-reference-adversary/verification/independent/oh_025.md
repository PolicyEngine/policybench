The engine gives the head's $6,500 of premiums no weight at all in Ohio. The cause is not a judgment that the
premiums were pre-tax. Worksheet line 3 is coded as "Medicare-eligible AND the employer paid none", but the
statute and Ohio's own worksheet (including the 2022 booklet the engine cites) say "eligible for Medicare
**OR** an employer-paid plan". The worksheet line 1 branch, meanwhile, reads an input
(`health_insurance_premiums`) that has had no formula since PR #8178. A third, latent defect would
double-count the line-8 amount for couples once it is nonzero, so the fix must cover it too.

No reading of the prompt that I consider reasonable yields the reference. The only route to $1,921.57 is to
infer that the $6,500 was paid with pre-tax dollars. The prompt states no such fact, gross wages enter
federal AGI in full, and the engine itself counts the same $6,500 as IRC 213 medical care on the federal
side.

## 2. Mapping the prompt's facts to the law

- **Head (61, no wages, "has employer-sponsored insurance"; spouse also has ESI).** Under (A)(10)(a) the
  head is "eligible to participate in any subsidized health plan maintained by any employer of the taxpayer
  or of the taxpayer's spouse". The full (a) deduction (worksheet line 1) is therefore barred, and the
  head's after-tax premiums go to **line 3**, where they are subject to the 7.5% floor. Line 3 also covers
  this case on its own terms: eligibility for an employer-paid plan alone suffices, with no Medicare
  requirement.
- **"health insurance premiums excluding Medicare Part B: $6,500" and "other health insurance premiums:
  $6,500".** These are one amount listed twice. The first label names a total (all premiums except Part B),
  and the second is the engine's component category ("premiums not otherwise represented by modeled
  Marketplace, CHIP, Medicaid, Medicare ... premiums", per `other_health_insurance_premiums.py:11-15`). In
  the engine, only `health_insurance_premiums_without_medicare_part_b` feeds the medical-expense premium
  aggregate (`medical_expense_health_insurance_premiums.py:17-25`). Reading them as additive ($13,000) is
  possible but strained (reading R3 below).
- **Were the $6,500 paid with pre-tax dollars?** No stated fact says so. The prompt instructs models to
  "treat any unlisted ... input as 0 and any other unlisted ... fact ... as false". The spouse's "gross
  wages" of $62,725 enter federal AGI in full ($94,925.29 in both the engine and every model), so no premium
  was excluded from wages. The engine's own federal path counts the same $6,500 as IRC 213 medical care:
  `itemized_medical_expenses` adds `medical_expense_health_insurance_premiums` and `other_medical_expenses`
  (`variables/gov/irs/income/taxable_income/deductions/itemizing/itemized_medical_expenses.py:25-28`). The
  head also has no wages, so the head cannot have a payroll deduction of their own.
  `pre_tax_health_insurance_premiums` exists, but it is unset, and in 2.15.17 nothing in `variables/gov/irs`,
  `variables/household` or `variables/input` references it. The premiums are therefore after-tax and count.
- **"employer sponsored insurance premiums: $21,208".** The engine defines this as "Annual employer-paid
  health insurance premiums. CBO treats this as part of household market income."
  (`variables/input/employer_sponsored_insurance_premiums.py:8-11`). The taxpayer did not pay it, and it is
  excluded from income under IRC 106, so it is outside (b) ("the amount the taxpayer paid"; "not otherwise
  ... excluded"). The prompt's own "health insurance premiums excluding Medicare Part B: $6,500" also
  implies that the head's total paid premiums are $6,500, which leaves the $21,208 out. Reading it as
  after-tax premiums the head paid (reading R4) is inconsistent with that total.
- **"over-the-counter health expenses: $50".** Under IRC 213(b), non-prescribed medicine is excluded. The
  label does not say whether the $50 bought drugs or non-drug supplies (Pub 502 allows, for example,
  bandages). The more natural reading is OTC medicine, which is excluded; the engine excludes OTC federally
  by design. Counting it is reading R2, worth $1.38.
- **"other medical expenses: $800"** goes on line 4.

**Why premiums are excluded.** Line 3 requires Medicare eligibility AND that the employer paid none; the law
requires Medicare eligibility OR eligibility for an employer-paid plan. Line 1 reads `health_insurance_premiums`,
which since PR #8178 has been a bare input. A person whose premiums arrive through the decomposed inputs
(the CPS/microcosm path, and this scenario) therefore gets $0 on line 1 even when eligible. For a
non-Medicare person, then, premiums reach neither line 1 nor line 3, whatever their ESI status.

**History, from main's (shallow) git log.** Commit c1b4bc5c71, "Decompose SPM MOOP premiums (#8178)", dated
2026-04-29, made two changes:
- It dropped `health_insurance_premiums`' `adds` of `[health_insurance_premiums_without_medicare_part_b,
  medicare_part_b_premiums]`.
- It changed line 4 from `medical_out_of_pocket_expenses` (= `health_insurance_premiums` +
  `other_medical_expenses`) to `other_medical_expenses` only.

Before #8178 this household would have had premiums counted twice: once in full on line 1 (because the
category defaults to NONE and `has_esi` was ignored) and once on line 4. #8178 removed premiums from line 4
without repairing line 3's gate or line 1's input, so the premiums vanished.

**Latent double count (this case masks it because line 8 = 0).** The person variable `adds` a TaxUnit
variable. policyengine-core's `adds` loop calls `self.calculate(added_variable, period,
map_to=variable.entity.key)` (`policyengine_core/simulations/simulation.py:1104-1110`), and `map_result`
for group to person with no `how` returns `entity_pop.project(values)` (`simulation.py:696-701`), which is a
broadcast. Every member of the tax unit therefore receives the full line-8 amount. `oh_agi` then sums the
person-level `oh_agi_person` values, so line 8 is subtracted once per member: twice for this couple, n times
for a unit of n. The proration variable `oh_insured_unreimbursed_medical_care_expenses_person.py:17-31`
already exists and is tested, but nothing uses it. No test on main exercises
`oh_unreimbursed_medical_care_expense_deduction` at the couple level.

## 6. Fix specification (policyengine-us; all three parts are required)

1. **Line 3**, in `oh_insured_unreimbursed_medical_care_expense_amount.py`. Replace the AND gate:
   ```python
   premiums = max_(
       person("medical_expense_health_insurance_premiums", period)
       - person("pre_tax_health_insurance_premiums", period),
       0,
   )  # R.C. 5747.01(A)(10)(b): "to the extent not otherwise deducted or excluded"
   line_3_eligible = person("is_medicare_eligible", period) | person(
       "oh_employer_subsidized_plan_eligible", period
   )
   return premiums * line_3_eligible + person("other_medical_expenses", period)
   ```
2. **Line 1**, in `oh_uninsured_unreimbursed_medical_care_expenses.py`. Read the same after-tax `premiums`
   (not the bare `health_insurance_premiums` input) and gate on
   `~is_medicare_eligible & ~oh_employer_subsidized_plan_eligible`. Per the worksheet's line 1 note, also
   reduce by the person's federal self-employed health insurance deduction.
3. **New helper** `oh_employer_subsidized_plan_eligible` (Person, bool). True when the head or spouse of the
   person's tax unit `has_esi` (statute: "any employer of the taxpayer or of the taxpayer's spouse"), or the
   person's `employer_contribution_to_health_insurance_premiums_category` is SOME or ALL. The category's
   default of NONE is a default rather than an observation, so it must not override `has_esi`.
   - Design assumption to confirm with the data owners: `pre_tax_health_insurance_premiums` is a subset of
     the reported premium total.
4. **Broadcast fix**, in `oh_unreimbursed_medical_care_expense_deduction_person.py:18`. Change `adds` from
   `oh_insured_unreimbursed_medical_care_expenses` (TaxUnit) to `oh_insured_unreimbursed_medical_care_expenses_person`.
   Without this, the scenario would compute a 361.21 deduction and tax of 1,911.64 instead of 1,916.60.
5. **Existing tests that encode the misreading and must change**:
   - `tests/.../insured_unreimbursed_expenses/oh_insured_unreimbursed_medical_care_expense_amount.yaml`, case
     2 (Medicare-eligible, category SOME): expected 600 becomes 1,100.
   - `integration.yaml`, case 2 (SOME, AGI 10,000): expected 0 becomes 1,100 − 750 = 350.
   - `integration.yaml`, case 7 (spouse category NA, both Medicare-eligible): expected 950 becomes
