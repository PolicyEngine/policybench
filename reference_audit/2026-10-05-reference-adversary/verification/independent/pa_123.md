# Independent verification: scenario_123, state_income_tax_before_refundable_credits (PA)

Verifier: independent Claude Opus 5.5 agent, 2026-10-06. Read-only apart from this file. No simulations were run (machine load ~260–600). Every engine claim below comes from reading source. Every number is either hand-computed here or read from a named artifact.

## Verdict: AMBIGUOUS

The reference ($3,070.06) is the correct PA-40 liability of the parents' joint return. The consensus ($4,451.56) is the correct total over every PA return the household must file, because the 16-year-old must file a PA-40 too. The text the models saw does not settle which of these two quantities the output means.

- Not CONFIRMED. The model-facing text does not plainly cover the child's return. The prompt renders a single "Tax unit:" block and says everyone is "in one household group for tax and benefit calculations". Careful readers split on scope in the sibling federal cell (8 parents-only, 15 two-return, 20 pooled; see section 3).
- Not REFUTED. The definition never limits itself to one return. The prompt asks for quantities "for this household" and says "Assume tax filing … when required". The child has a legal PA filing requirement.
- Of the adversary's two remedies, the exclusion is supported. A recompute to $4,451.56 is not, because it only moves the penalty from one reasonable reader to the other.

Root cause: a scenario-generation and definition-scope gap, not an engine defect (section 5).

## 2. Law

**Rate.** 72 P.S. § 7302(a) (tax years from 1/1/2024, Act 2023), read on codes.findlaw.com this session: "a tax upon each dollar of income received by that resident … at the rate of three and seven hundredths per cent." The 2025 PA-40 IN (rev. 04-25, pre-freeze), p. 1: "The state income tax rate for 2025 is 3.07 percent (0.0307)."

**The child must file.** 2025 PA-40 IN, p. 3 (pdftotext of the official pa.gov PDF, read this session):
- Who must file: "You received total PA gross taxable income in excess of $33 during 2025, even if no tax is due."
- Minors: "PA law does not exempt a minor from the previously described requirements to file a PA tax return even if claimed as a dependent on a federal return. A parent/guardian must file a return on behalf of the minor child in such circumstances."
- PA DOR, PIT Guide, Tax Forgiveness (pa.gov, read this session): "The dependent child with taxable income in excess of $33 must file a PA-40 … and a PA-40 Schedule SP."

**The child's income cannot go on the parents' return.**
- PA joint filing is "You and your spouse … can file a joint return" (PA-40 IN, filing status J). "The income and losses of a taxpayer and spouse must be determined separately."
- PA has no equivalent of federal Form 8814, and Form 8814 covers only unearned income anyway.
- Federally, 26 U.S.C. § 73(a): "Amounts received in respect of the services of a child shall be included in his gross income and not in the gross income of the parent."
- So reading C, a pooled $145,002 joint base, is wrong in law. It only matches B numerically in PA.

**Amounts.**
- Parents' PA-40: $100,000 + $1 + $1 = $100,002 × 0.0307 = **$3,070.06**.
- Child's PA-40: $45,000 × 0.0307 = **$1,381.50**. This matches the conformance scan's engine own-return value (`definition_conformance.md:30`).
- Tax forgiveness is $0 on both returns. The DOR page gives a married-with-one-dependent table running from $22,500 (100%) to $24,750 (10%), and the parents have about $100,002 of eligibility income. A dependent child "may be eligible to claim tax forgiveness only if … the child's parents … are eligible" (DOR page). If the child were treated as a non-dependent, their $45,000 is far above the single table too.
- Household total: **$4,451.56**.

**Does dependency status matter?** The child is a qualifying child under 26 U.S.C. § 152(c)(1):
- Age under 19 (§ 152(c)(3)(A)(i)).
- Lives with the parents.
- The support test, § 152(c)(1)(D) ("has not provided over one-half of such individual's own support"), is unlisted. Under the prompt's "unlisted fact = false" convention, the child did not self-support.

The PA parents' return is $3,070.06 whether or not the child is a dependent: PA has no dependency exemption, and forgiveness is $0 either way. The child's PA tax is $1,381.50 either way. So the state cell turns only on scope. The federal cell additionally uses dependency for the $2,200 CTC.

**Federal check, by hand.**
- Parents: $100,002 − $32,200 (2026 MFJ standard deduction) − $2,000 (§ 170(p) non-itemizer charitable deduction) = $65,802. Tax: $2,480 + 12% × $41,002 = $7,400.24. Less the $2,200 CTC: **$5,200.24**, equal to the reference. So the federal reference is parents-only too.
- Child's own federal return: $45,000 − $16,100 = $28,900. Tax: $1,240 + 12% × $16,500 = **$3,220**.
- Household total: **$8,420.24**.

## 4. Engine path (policyengine-us 2.15.17; main 2.29.11 identical in logic)

All paths are under `adds0928-stage2/.venv/.../policyengine_us/`.

1. `scenarios.py:513-539` (PolicyBench) builds a single `tax_unit` with every member, child included. `ground_truth.py` sums the TaxUnit-entity output over that one unit.
2. `variables/household/demographic/tax_unit/is_tax_unit_dependent.py:10-13` defines a dependent as `~head & ~spouse`, so the child is a dependent.
3. `variables/gov/irs/income/taxable_income/adjusted_gross_income/irs_gross_income/irs_gross_income.py:16-19`: `total += not_dependent * max_(0, add(person, period, [source]))`. Dependents' income is zeroed out of the unit's gross income, which is correct for the parents' return.
4. `variables/gov/states/pa/tax/income/taxable_income/pa_total_taxable_income.py:13-18` computes `add(tax_unit, period, ["irs_gross_income"]) − PA nontaxable sources`. That gives $100,002.
5. `pa_adjusted_taxable_income.py:13-14` subtracts only the 529 deduction (`deductions/pa_tax_deductions.py:16`). `forgiveness/pa_income_tax_before_forgiveness.py:13-16` multiplies by `gov.states.pa.tax.income.rate`, which is 0.0307 (`parameters/.../pa/tax/income/rate.yaml`). Result: $3,070.06.
6. `forgiveness/pa_tax_forgiveness_rate.py:13-33` returns 0 at this eligibility income. Then `pa_income_tax_after_forgiveness.py:13-14` → `pa_income_tax_before_refundable_credits.py:12` → `variables/gov/states/tax/income/state_income_tax_before_refundable_credits.py:10` (adds the state list).
7. **No variable computes a dependent's own return inside the parents' unit.** The only own-return machinery makes the person head of a separate tax unit with the input `claimed_as_dependent_on_another_return` (`variables/household/demographic/person/claimed_as_dependent_on_another_return.py`, an input with no formula; `tax_unit/head_is_dependent_elsewhere.py:11-17`). That is how the conformance scan computed $1,381.50 and $3,220 (`definition_conformance.md:44`).
8. **Upstream main (2.29.11):** `irs_gross_income.py` and `is_tax_unit_dependent.py` are byte-identical. The PA files differ only in their `reference` URLs (diffed this session). Upgrading would not move the cell.

Conclusion: the engine correctly computes reading A. It cannot express reading B without a second tax unit, which the PolicyBench builder never creates.

## 5. Root cause and shared cells

**Root cause: a scenario-generation plus definition-scope gap.** It is not an engine defect.

- The generator keeps households with one *data* tax unit (`scenarios.py:1035-1055`; paper `index.qmd:1055`).
- The enhanced-CPS unit puts a 16-year-old earning $45,000 in the parents' unit as a dependent. In law that household files two returns, federal and PA.
- The output definitions are silent on which returns count. The prompt mixes a one-unit signal ("one household group for tax … calculations", "Tax unit:") with household-level cues ("for this household", "Assume tax filing … when required", household payroll tax).
- The engine's parents' return is right in law, so this is not an engine defect.

**Every scenario where a non-head/spouse member has income.** My own scan of all 100 US `scenario_json` rows for income-type inputs found three:

| Scenario | Person | Income | Must file own return? | Cells affected (reference → reading-B value) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| scenario_123 (PA) | Child 1, 16 | $45,000 wages | Yes (federal and PA) | state $3,070.06 → **$4,451.56**; federal $5,200.24 → **$8,420.24** |
| scenario_093 (MO) | "Dependent 1", 27, `is_disabled` | $45,000 wages | Yes (federal; MO per scan) | state $3,388.25 → **$4,528.08** (+$1,139.83 MO own return, engine value from the conformance scan, not hand-verified); federal $8,628.99 → **$11,848.99** (+$3,220) |
| scenario_067 (IN) | Dependent 1, 23 | $10,800 disability benefits | No (outside gross income per scan) | none |

The own-return refundable credits and local income tax are $0 for both filers (conformance JSON), so those cells do not move. All four affected cells are currently scored.

Model results on scenario_093: state, 0 exact under either reading; federal, 1 exact on the reference (gpt-6-luna) and 0 on reading B.

scenario_093 has an extra issue I did not resolve. A 27-year-old with $45,000 of wages is a qualifying child only if permanently and totally disabled (§ 152(c)(3)(B)). They fail the qualifying-relative gross-income test. The prompt's general "is disabled" flag does not settle § 152, so the dependency status itself rests on an unlisted fact. That deserves its own check: it may affect the parents' federal and MO returns beyond the scope question.

The diagnosis judge is built to treat references as correct (`docs/audit.md:70-80`), so this is not an independent ruling.
- **`docs/audit.md:79`** (this pass, commit bec3b244) already says "the income tax references left out a dependent's own return". That is the pass author's framing, not an adjudication.
- **Rules 3 and 4 of `reference_audit/2026-09-28/README.md`.**
  - Rule 3, "The benchmark's output definitions hold", justified recomputing Maryland, where the definition plainly excluded local tax. Here the definition is not plain, so Rule 3 does not compel a recompute.
  - Rule 4 and the exclusion rule exclude when "a careful reader could take the stated facts the other way". That reasoning fits this cell, but the rule's literal trigger is an *unlisted engine input*. A scope ambiguity needs a new reason code, such as `definition_scope_ambiguous`, matching the adversary pipeline's `definition_exclusion`.

## 7. Recommendation: exclude, do not regenerate

**Exclude all four cells together:** scenario_123 state and federal, and scenario_093 state and federal.

- Mark them `scored=false` with reason `definition_scope_ambiguous`.
- Record both values: the reading-A reference and the reading-B alternative (values in section 5).
- Excluding only the flagged state cell would be inconsistent. The federal cells have the same scope question and are scored today. 1 model loses a federal hit on scenario_123 and 1 on scenario_093.

Reasons:
1. The model-facing text admits both readings, and careful models visibly split (section 3). Recomputing to $4,451.56 would just penalize the other reasonable reader, and would reward 16 models for a pooling error.
2. The reference is right for the quantity the engine computes, the parents' return. There is no engine defect to fix, so `reference_engine_defect` and "regenerated" do not apply.
3. Regenerating the scenario would change prompts and require re-running 46 models. That breaks the frozen-run practice: excluded outputs stay in the payload so every prompt and response stays inspectable.
4. Picking reading A or B for future runs is a methodology choice the definition leaves open, so it belongs with Max.

For the next run:
- Either state the scope in both income-tax definitions ("the head/spouse tax unit's return; exclude any separate return a dependent files", or "the total over every return household members must file") or add the UK-style "requested outputs are household totals" line to the US prompt.
- Add a generation filter that drops or flags households where a non-head/spouse member has a federal (IRC 6012) or state filing requirement. PA's $33 threshold catches almost any dependent with income. Alternatively, build the dependent's own return as a second tax unit with `claimed_as_dependent_on_another_return`.
