Laws 2026, Ch. 140 (HB 4168) was approved and filed on June 13, 2026, before the 2026-07-03 freeze. It
rewrote 43-1041(A)(1) to read "$15,750, subject to subsection H of this section". It left subsection H
untouched. Subsection H requires the department to index that amount "in the same manner in which the
federal basic standard deduction is adjusted for inflation pursuant to section 63". The federal basic
standard deduction for a single filer has the same $15,750 base. 26 U.S.C. 63(c)(7)(B)(ii) indexes it for
taxable years after 2025 from a 2024 base, and Rev. Proc. 2025-32 (October 2025) publishes the 2026 result
as $16,100. Arizona's base equals the federal base and the indexing method is the same, so the 2026 Arizona
single amount is $16,100. That result does not depend on the C-CPI-U vintage (section 1.4).

The engine returns $15,750 for 2026 because the `uprating: gov.irs.uprating` key sits on the parameter's
parent node. The parent has no `propagate_metadata_to_children` flag, and policyengine-core only uprates
leaf parameters whose own metadata carries `uprating`. The YAML comment says so directly: "This parent
uprating remains inactive pending selective propagation in PolicyEngine/policyengine-core#537."

I could not construct a reasonable reading that yields $15,750 for 2026 (section 1.5). The verdict is
therefore CONFIRMED, not AMBIGUOUS.

### 1.4 The federal method, and why it gives exactly $16,100 for Arizona

- 26 U.S.C. 63(c)(7) (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/63, fetched 2026-10-06; as amended by
  P.L. 119-21, enacted 2025-07-04):
  - (A): "(i) by substituting '$23,625' for '$4,400' in subparagraph (B), and (ii) by substituting '$15,750'
    for '$3,000' in subparagraph (C)."
  - (B)(ii): "In the case of a taxable year beginning after 2025, the $23,625 and $15,750 amounts in
    subparagraph (A) shall each be increased by an amount equal to— (I) such dollar amount, multiplied by
    (II) the cost-of-living adjustment determined under section 1(f)(3) for the calendar year in which the
    taxable year begins, determined by substituting '2024' for '2016' in subparagraph (A)(ii) thereof. If
    any increase under this clause is not a multiple of $50, such increase shall be rounded to the next
    lowest multiple of $50."
- 26 U.S.C. 1(f)(3)(C) (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1): "For purposes of any provision of
  this title which provides for the substitution of a year after 2016 for '2016' in subparagraph (A)(ii),
  subparagraph (A) shall be applied by substituting 'the C-CPI-U for calendar year 2016' for 'the CPI for
  calendar year 2016' and all that follows in clause (ii) thereof." So the COLA is the C-CPI-U for the
  12 months ending August 2025 divided by the C-CPI-U for the 12 months ending August 2024, minus 1.
- Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section 4.14(1) (https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf; PDF creation date
  2025-10-17, pre-freeze): "For taxable years beginning in 2026, the standard deduction amounts under
  § 63(c)(2) are as follows: Married Individuals Filing Joint Returns and Surviving Spouses $32,200; Heads of
  Households $24,150; Unmarried Individuals ... $16,100; Married Individuals Filing Separate Returns
  $16,100." Section 4.04(3) of the same document: "the amount used in § 23(a)(4) to determine the amount of
  the credit under § 23 that may be refundable is $5,120."

The single amount does not depend on the CPI vintage. The IRS's 2026 COLA `x` is bounded by its own
published figures, all of which use the same 2024-base COLA:

- Single $16,100: 15,750(1+x) ∈ [16,100, 16,150), so x ∈ [0.022222, 0.025397).
- HOH $24,150: 23,625(1+x) ∈ [24,150, 24,200), so x ∈ [0.022222, 0.024339). The IRS evidently rounds the
  resulting amount down to a $50 multiple, because $525 is not a multiple of $50.
- Adoption refundable portion $5,120: 26 U.S.C. 23(h)(3) uses the 2024 base, and 23(h)(2) rounds to the
  nearest $10. So 5,000(1+x) ∈ [5,115, 5,125), and x ∈ [0.023, 0.025).
- Intersection: x ∈ [0.023, 0.024339).

Applying that range to Arizona's bases:

- AZ single and MFS: 15,750(1+x) ∈ [16,112, 16,133), which rounds down to **$16,100** for every admissible x.
- AZ HOH: 23,625(1+x) ∈ [24,168, 24,200), which gives **$24,150** for every admissible x.
- AZ joint is the one amount with a residual question, and **no scored cell depends on it**. On the
  federal-match reading, the federal joint amount is 200% of the single amount under §63(c)(2)(A), so it
  is $32,200. Every year from 2021 to 2025, ADOR published Arizona amounts identical to the federal ones
  (see the engine YAML history and its ADOR booklet references). On the direct reading, $31,500 × (1+x)
  ranges over [32,224.5, 32,266.7). That rounds down to $32,200 if x < 0.0238095 and to $32,250 otherwise.
  - Using today's BLS values (series SUUR0000SA0, fetched 2026-10-06 via api.bls.gov), x = 0.024217, which
    gives $32,250.
  - Using the older-vintage C-CPI-U values stored in the engine
    (`parameters/gov/bls/cpi/c_cpi_u.yaml`), x = 0.023226, which gives $32,200.
  - A hybrid (final 2023 months plus the engine's later months) gives x = 0.023685, which gives $32,200.
  - The statutory vintage is set by §1(f)(6)(A): "the latest values so published as of the date on which
    such Bureau publishes the initial value ... for the month of August". I did not reconstruct it.

  I recommend $32,200 (federal match, and ADOR's consistent practice) and flag $32,250 as the alternative.

### 1.5 Readings that could yield the reference ($15,750 for 2026), and why each fails

1. **"ADOR had not published a 2026 figure before the freeze."** True; see 1.6. But subsection H fixes
   the method ("shall adjust ... in the same manner"), and the federal 2026 output of that method was
   published in October 2025. That the department had not yet computed the figure does not make the
   unadjusted face amount the 2026 law. A.R.S. 43-1041(A)(1) itself says the amount is "subject to
   subsection H".
2. **"Index from a 2025 base, so the first increase comes in 2027."** This conflicts with "in the same
   manner": federal law indexes the identical $15,750 TY2025 amount from a 2024 base, so the first increase
   falls in 2026. It also conflicts with the statute's own pattern. The pre-HB 4168 amounts ($12,200,
   $18,350, $24,400) were the 2019 federal amounts, indexed "for each taxable year beginning from and after
   December 31, 2019", that is, first adjusted in 2020. The ADOR amounts the engine records for 2021 to
   2024, sourced from ADOR booklets, equal the federal amounts for those years.
3. **"The legislature meant $15,750 as a fixed number."** The enacted text retains "subject to subsection
   H". Neither summary says the amount is frozen.

None of these is a reasonable second reading, so the verdict is not AMBIGUOUS.
