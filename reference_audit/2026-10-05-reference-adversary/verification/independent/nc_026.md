### 1c. Federal law on the two groups

Retrieved from the eCFR versioner API as of 2026-07-01, and from the U.S. Code via Cornell LII. All texts predate the freeze.

- **42 CFR 435.218** [77 FR 17205, Mar. 23, 2012]: "(a) Basis. This section implements section 1902(a)(10)(A)(ii)(XX) of the Act. (b) Eligibility —(1) Criteria. The agency may provide Medicaid to individuals who: (i) Are under age 65; (ii) Are not eligible for and enrolled for mandatory coverage ...; (iii) Are not otherwise eligible for and enrolled for optional coverage under a State's Medicaid State plan in accordance with section 1902(a)(10)(A)(ii)(I) through (XIX) of the Act ...; and (iv) Have household income that exceeds 133 percent FPL but is at or below the income standard elected by the agency and approved in its Medicaid State plan". The regulation contains no other-coverage condition.
- **SSA 1902(a)(10)(A)(ii)(XX)**, 42 U.S.C. 1396a (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1396a): "beginning January 1, 2014, who are under 65 years of age and are not described in or enrolled under a previous subclause of this clause, and whose income ... exceeds 133 percent of the poverty line ... but does not exceed the highest income eligibility level established under the State plan or under a waiver of the plan, subject to subsection (hh)". An insured child is not "described in" subclause (XIV), "optional targeted low-income children described in section 1396d(u)(2)(B)", because the OTLIC definition requires no other coverage. So the insured child falls into (XX). Subsection 1902(hh) only permits phase-in and limits parents; it does not restrict children.
- **42 CFR 435.229** [81 FR 86454, Nov. 30, 2016]: "The agency may provide Medicaid to individuals under age 19 ... who meet the definition of an optional targeted low-income child in § 435.4 and have household income at or below the income standard established by the agency".
- **42 CFR 435.4**, "Optional targeted low-income child", paragraph (2): "An optional targeted low-income child is not covered under a group health plan or health insurance coverage ...". The adversary quoted this correctly. It governs only the 435.229 group.
- **42 CFR 435.118(c)(2):** the maximum standard for ages 6–18 is "the higher of— (i) 133 percent FPL; (ii) The highest effective income level ... as of March 23, 2010 or December 31, 2013". NC's standard is 133% (SPA p. 10).
- **42 CFR 435.603(d)(4):** "a state must subtract an amount equivalent to 5 percentage points of the Federal poverty level for the applicable family size only to determine the eligibility of an individual for medical assistance under the eligibility group with the highest income standard using MAGI-based methodologies in the applicable Title of the Act, but not to determine eligibility for a particular eligibility group." For an insured 6–18 child in NC, the highest-standard Title XIX group available is 435.218 at 211%, so the effective cutoff is 216%.

## 3. The April 1 FPL switch

- **Law.** G.S. 108A-54.3A(b) moves NC to the new guideline each April 1. Under the prompt's constant-income convention, each child fails January–March 2026 (2025 FPG; 213.6% net) and passes April–December 2026 (2026 FPG; 207.8% net). That is 9 of 12 months.
- **Continuous eligibility.** Under SSA 1902(e)(12) (12-month continuous eligibility for children under 19), a child found eligible in April 2026 stays eligible for 12 months.
- **Engine.** `fpg()` (`variables/gov/hhs/tax_unit_fpg.py:4-8`) reads `parameters(period).gov.hhs.fpg` at the start of the annual period. `parameters/gov/hhs/fpg.yaml:17` (`2026-01-01: 15_960`) and `:75` (`2026-01-01: 5_680`) apply the 2026 guideline to the whole of 2026. The engine therefore models no January–March window.
- **Effect on the 0/1 output.** The output definition asks "whether Child N is eligible for Medicaid under PolicyEngine rules". It does not ask whether the child is eligible in every month. Under the benchmark's annual convention, eligibility for most of the year, including the whole of the year's guideline cycle from April, gives 1.
  - A reader who required all 12 months would get 0. That is a benchmark-wide convention question about annual flags near a threshold in April-switch states, not the defect the adversary alleged.
  - It applies identically to child3, where the adversary itself kept the reference.
  - None of the 44 models answered 0 on this ground. Their explanations cite a 133% or 138% limit, ESI, or misstated income.
  - I treat the switch as a caveat, not a reason to flip.
