# Review of the judge's texts for release dashboard-data-20261111

This page records an independent reading of the judge's texts for release dashboard-data-20261111, which adds Claude Fable 5.2 as the board's 52nd model, and what the reading found. The session that operated the release runs on Claude Fable 5.2, so PolicyBench had other models read the texts and made no discretionary change to references or scoring on their findings.

## What was reviewed and by whom

PolicyBench scores models' answers to questions on 100 households in tax year 2026 against references computed by policyengine-us 2.60.0. For every cell (one household and one output) that some model answered wrong, the judge, Claude Opus 5.5, writes a case note and a sentence for each wrong row, each with a class. For this release the judge wrote new texts for the 122 cells Claude Fable 5.2 answered wrong, and they replace the published texts for every model in those cells.

Two reads covered the judged cells.

- **First read, 2026-11-11.** Six Claude Opus 5.5 readers, the same model as the judge, split the 122 re-judged cells. Each case file held the reference, any decision on record, the notes of release 20261027 and of this release, and every new row with its answer and class, with Claude Fable 5.2's rows marked. The readers checked for unanswered doubts about the reference or the prompt, the law each diagnosis rests on, the fit of each class to its text, equal treatment of Claude Fable 5.2's rows, and hygiene. Their brief set the law as tax year 2026 as enacted by 2026-07-03 and said the engine's own conventions are not errors.
- **Second read, 2026-11-11.** GPT-6.1 Sol readers read, in 14 units, the 559 judged cells Claude Fable 5.2 answered right, where it has no row, with the same checks less the row comparison. Their brief added three conventions: a 2026 amount not published by 2026-07-03 takes the last one published; the prompt's rule that an unlisted fact is false sits beside PolicyBench's exclusions of cells where "a careful reader could take the stated facts the other way"; and a yes-or-no output asks what PolicyEngine returns under its definition. The brief also said answers match to the cent. That was wrong: the scorer counts an amount as right within $1 ([benchmark card](../benchmark_card.md)).

Readers in both reads checked legal facts against primary sources (statutes, regulations, agency pages, enrolled bills) within limits per case and per unit: the first read used 111 lookups, and all 14 second-read units report web access. Neither read saw the prompts.

The doubts about a reference or a prompt (P1s, defined below) then went to adjudicators, who worked from the case's prompt, the engine's code, the law and PolicyBench's record of decisions, and ran no engine.

- **Round one, 2026-11-11.** Claude Opus 5.5 adjudicators took 13 causes covering 21 of the first read's 24 P1s, plus one cause from two P3 findings on employer insurance premiums and a saver's credit. A GPT-6.1 Sol reviewer tried to refute each of the 14 and adjudicated two more P1s on its own, for 16 items. The last P1, on SNAP farm rent in scenario_112, concerns an output the release does not score, and PolicyBench classed it text only.
- **Round two, 2026-11-12.** Claude Opus 5.5 adjudicators took the ten second-read units that hold a P1, and all ten are complete, covering the 21 P1s. The adjudications of the last three units to finish, which hold 12 of the 21, were written under the scorer's rule that a dollar answer is right within $1; the brief for round one and for the first seven units stated the cent rule. No refutation pass by another lab has run on this round.

Both reviewing models have rows on the board. GPT-6.1 Sol stated in each refutation that it is an OpenAI model.

## What this release does with the findings

This release changes no reference and no scored output on these findings. That holds for the four questions the errata check raised on scored cells too, although replacing the reference with the other reading's value in `scenario_026 child1_chip_eligible` or `scenario_100 tanf` would raise Claude Fable 5.2's score, as would $119.38 in `scenario_095 state_refundable_credits`. The first read covered only cells Claude Fable 5.2 answered wrong, so acting on it alone would favour that model; the second read covers the cells it answered right, so that the sample runs both ways. PolicyBench will decide the open questions from both reads together, for every model, in a later release. Apart from one Minnesota output, `scenario_122 state_refundable_credits`, which PolicyBench decided on 2026-11-01 to stop scoring, the release keeps release 20261027's references and scored set. The judge's texts are published as written, and the statements the reviews found wrong are listed in [the judge-text errata](judge_text_errata.md).

## The car-loan pattern

In 21 households the prompt lists auto loan interest above zero and states nothing about the vehicle or the loan. The federal deduction for qualified passenger vehicle loan interest (26 U.S.C. 163(h)(4), added by P.L. 119-21) covers interest on a loan taken out after 2024 for a new vehicle with final assembly in the United States, secured by a first lien. The engine allows the deduction only from a separate input for qualifying interest that no scenario sets, so every reference denies it. One reading applies the prompt's rule that "any other unlisted household fact, boolean, or status input" is false, and keeps the references. The other takes the listing as a statement that the loan qualifies, since, as a first-read reader noted, the line has no other use on a federal return. The Claude Opus 5.5 adjudication and the GPT-6.1 Sol refutation both class the pattern as a prompt that leaves a needed fact open.

Entering each household's stated interest as qualifying interest and rerunning policyengine-us 2.60.0 moves 15 cells by more than $1. Eleven are scored: `federal_income_tax_before_refundable_credits` in scenarios 003, 018, 053, 067, 086, 099 and 101; `federal_refundable_credits` in 026 and 028; and `state_income_tax_before_refundable_credits` in 053 (Idaho) and 067 (Indiana). The other four (federal income tax in 002, 033 and 093, local income tax in 067) are already unscored and stay so below. In the 11 scored cells, 81 model answers match only the reference and 22 only the other reading's value; Claude Fable 5.2 matches the other reading in 7 and the reference in 1.

Each scoring uses PolicyBench's headline score: within each household, the weighted share of scored outputs a model answered right (an amount within $1 of the reference, a yes-or-no output exactly), averaged over the 100 households with equal weight, with output weights taken from population data.

- **(b) Excluded:** the 11 cells removed for every model, leaving 1,923 scored outputs.
- **(c) Other reading:** the 11 cells scored against the values the engine gives when the stated interest counts as qualifying, 1,934 scored outputs.
